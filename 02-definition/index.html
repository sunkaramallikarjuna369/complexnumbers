📐 Definition of Complex Numbers

The Structure z = a + bi

Basic Definition

A complex number is defined as:

z = a + bi

Where:

  • a is the real part (Re(z))
  • b is the imaginary part (Im(z))
  • i is the imaginary unit (i² = −1)
  • a, b ∈ ℝ (both are real numbers)

Examples

z₁ = 3 + 4i

Real part: 3, Imaginary part: 4


z₂ = 1 − 2i

Real part: 1, Imaginary part: −2


z₃ = 5 (purely real)

Real part: 5, Imaginary part: 0


z₄ = 3i (purely imaginary)

Real part: 0, Imaginary part: 3

The Imaginary Unit i

The imaginary unit i is defined by:

i² = −1

This means: i = √(−1)

Powers of i

i⁰ = 1

i¹ = i

i² = −1

i³ = −i

i⁴ = 1

i⁵ = i (pattern repeats)

The powers of i cycle with period 4!

Why i?

The imaginary unit allows us to:

  • Solve equations like x² + 1 = 0
  • Represent rotations mathematically
  • Describe quantum states
  • Model oscillations and waves

Components

Real Part

Re(z) = a

The real part is the horizontal component in the complex plane.

Imaginary Part

Im(z) = b

The imaginary part is the vertical component in the complex plane.

Note: Im(z) is a real number (it's the coefficient of i)!

Example: z = 3 + 4i

Re(z) = 3

Im(z) = 4 (not 4i!)

The complex number has two real components!

Special Cases

Real Numbers

When b = 0:

z = a + 0i = a

All real numbers are complex numbers with imaginary part zero.

Imaginary Numbers

When a = 0:

z = 0 + bi = bi

Purely imaginary numbers lie on the imaginary axis.

Zero

When a = 0 and b = 0:

z = 0 + 0i = 0

Zero is both real and imaginary!

Number Systems

ℕ ⊂ ℤ ⊂ ℚ ⊂ ℝ ⊂ ℂ

Natural → Integers → Rationals → Reals → Complex

Complex numbers are the most general!

Quantum Computing Connection

In quantum computing, complex numbers are essential:

Quantum State Amplitudes

A qubit state is represented as:

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩

Where α and β are complex numbers!

Why Complex?

  • Phase information: Complex numbers encode both magnitude and phase
  • Interference: Quantum interference requires complex amplitudes
  • Unitary evolution: Quantum gates are complex matrices
  • Measurement: Probabilities come from |amplitude|²

Example: Superposition

|ψ⟩ = (1/√2)|0⟩ + (i/√2)|1⟩

α = 1/√2 (real)

β = i/√2 (imaginary)

Both amplitudes are complex numbers!

The relative phase between them matters!
